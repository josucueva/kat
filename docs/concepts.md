# Concepts' definitions

## Software

Software is an evolving system of executable instructions, data, configurations, and associated artifacts that together produce behavior and provide value to users or other systems.

In KAT, software is not considered only as source code. It is a system composed of behavior, structure, intent, constraints, decisions, and artifacts that change throughout its lifecycle.

---

## Knowledge

Knowledge is the structured understanding required to describe, reason about, modify, and validate a software system.

In KAT, knowledge includes information about the purpose of a system, its requirements, architecture, design decisions, dependencies, constraints, behaviors, and historical evolution.

Knowledge is what allows a software system to be understood beyond its current implementation.

---

## Evolution

Evolution is the continuous process through which a software system changes over time in response to new requirements, environmental changes, defects, c>onstraints, and improvements.

Software evolution includes both intentional changes, such as adding capabilities or refactoring, and unintentional changes, such as adaptations required to maintain compatibility or correctness.

---

## Intent

Intent is the purpose, motivation, or desired outcome that originates a change or defines the existence of a software element.

Intent explains why a requirement, architectural decision, component, or behavior exists.

---

## Artifact

An artifact is a concrete representation or output produced during the development, operation, or maintenance of a software system.

Examples include source code, documentation, tests, configurations, deployment definitions, diagrams, and executable binaries.

In KAT, artifacts are materializations of underlying knowledge rather than the primary source of truth.

---

## Consistency

Consistency is the property that a software system's knowledge, structure, constraints, and artifacts do not contain contradictions.

A consistent system maintains valid relationships between its elements and does not violate explicitly defined rules.

---

## Validation

Validation is the process of evaluating whether a software system satisfies its intended requirements, constraints, and expected properties.

Validation determines whether the system is aligned with its purpose and whether its current state remains acceptable as it evolves.

## Traceability

Traceability is the ability to establish, maintain, and navigate relationships between elements of a software system across different abstractions and throughout their lifecycle.

In KAT, traceability connects the intentions, requirements, decisions, structures, implementations, artifacts, validations, and operational evidence that compose a software system.

Traceability enables understanding of the origin, purpose, impact, and consequences of changes within a software system.

Examples of traceability include:

* A requirement traced to the architectural decisions that satisfy it.
* An architectural decision traced to the components that implement it.
* A component traced to the tests that validate its behavior.
* A change traced to the knowledge and artifacts affected by it.
